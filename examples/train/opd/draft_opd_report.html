Draft-OPD on the Gemma DSpark draft

Mengmeng Ji · 2026-11-09 · on-policy distillation (Draft-OPD, arXiv 2605.29343) applied to our DSpark draft, 2026-10-21 → 11-06. Full logs in examples/train/opd/README.md, branch mengmengj/draft-opd.

Conclusion. Draft-OPD does not improve our draft on held-out benchmarks at any seed checkpoint. On a converged seed every OPD run lowered accept length (1–3% after one round, up to 18% after eight). On an under-trained seed OPD gained 1–2%, but ordinary SFT on the exact same on-policy rollouts gained more (2–4%). In every paired comparison plain SFT ≥ OPD. We reproduce the paper's in-domain gain on its own prompt pool; it does not transfer. Decision: no OPD stage in the GLM-5.3 DFlash2 plan. The served draft stays block-12 epoch 33 at k=12.

1. What we tested

Draft-OPD trains the draft on its own speculative-decoding rollouts: run the draft+target loop on a prompt pool, then apply a forward KL to the target on accepted slots and a reverse KL on the first rejected slot, so the draft is corrected exactly where it failed. The paper reports +8% accept length (5.51 → 5.96) on Qwen3-4B with a DFlash draft, evaluated in-domain.

Our draft is a 5-layer all-sliding DSpark with a DFlash2 convolution, 32k draft vocabulary, block 12, trained 25 epochs at block 8 on 782k Gemma-regenerated conversations and 8 more at block 12 (served at k=12: 7.19 / 4.64 / 5.80 accept length on AIME / GPQA / LiveCodeBench). The question was whether OPD is a worthwhile final stage for it, and by extension for the GLM-5.3 draft.

3. What the sweep rules out

  • Pool domain. The science/chat pool was our first suspect. Rebuilding the paper's own math/code pool (hash-disjoint from SFT data) made the seed strong on the pool (0.44 vs 0.21) and still lost on benchmarks.
  • Forgetting. Mixing 8k SFT rows per round (ordinary loss) into OPD training cancelled the pool gain and lost more on benchmarks than OPD alone.
  • Loss details. λ_rej = 1 (the paper's value) collapses the draft in one round on every seed and pool: the reverse KL at a rejected slot is 17–19 nats against 0.05 on accepted slots, so at λ 1 the gradient is almost entirely "spread the draft out where it is wrong". The paper's γ decay changes nothing; forward KL on rejected slots is worse than reverse (matches the paper); dropping fully-accepted blocks is worse.
  • Seed convergence. A slightly earlier checkpoint (ep28) behaves like ep33. A genuinely under-trained checkpoint (ep8 of 26, 0.55 below final) does gain from one OPD round, but plain SFT on the same rollouts gains more. The gain is the low-LR anneal of a high-LR checkpoint plus one pass of on-policy text; the OPD objective subtracts from it by the same ~1% it does on the converged seed.
  • Rounds. On a converged seed the loss grows with rounds (v2: ckpt 34 flat, ckpt 41 −14…−18%; track A: −1…−3% at round 1, −5…−7% at round 2). The control also drifts down at round 2 (−4…−5%), so a narrow 16k pool is itself mildly harmful to a converged draft; OPD is below the control at every round.

Not tested: λ_rej between 0.05 and 1, below 0.05, or λ_acc ≠ 1; more than one round on the under-trained seed. At λ→0 OPD becomes forward-KL distillation on accepted slots only (it never trains on the first wrong token), so that end cannot beat SFT either; an interior optimum above SFT is possible but nothing in the data points to one.

4. Reading

The paper's result and ours are consistent. They train on math/code prompts and evaluate on math/code; we train on the same prompts and see the same in-domain gain, then evaluate on AIME, GPQA and LiveCodeBench and see the general distribution pay for it. The acceptance-aware objective sharpens the draft on the pool's prompts at the expense of everything else, and plain SFT on the identical rollouts is a strictly better use of the same data in every paired run we have.

Two of our conditions make the rejected-slot term harsher than the paper's: a 32k draft vocabulary (the target's full distribution at a rejected slot is mapped onto it, with 11–28% of first-rejected tokens out of vocabulary) and a hard-label-trained seed that is sharply overconfident where it is wrong. A full-vocab DFlash2 draft with a softer seed, as planned for GLM-5.3, would sit closer to the paper's regime. Even so, the under-trained-seed test was the condition most favourable to OPD and plain SFT still won, so we are not budgeting an OPD stage for GLM.

5. Decision for the GLM-5.3 draft

Do not use Draft-OPD on the GLM-5.3 DFlash2 draft. Train it with the SFT recipe to convergence, with the learning-rate anneal run to the end, and serve that checkpoint. On a converged draft OPD lost accept length in every configuration we tried, so it is not a cheap final step: the pool and pipeline exist, but running them on the finished draft is expected to make it worse, not better. On an under-trained draft OPD does gain, but plain SFT on the same on-policy rollouts gains more, so there is no checkpoint at which OPD is the better use of the data.

Two narrow uses remain. If a GLM run has to be stopped before its anneal, one round of on-policy rollouts plus ordinary SFT on them is a half-hour recovery of part of the gap, and the pipeline does that as is. And if there is slack after the draft is trained, one pilot round of OPD against a plain-SFT control on the same rollouts, with benchmark evals, costs about a GPU-day and would confirm the verdict for a full-vocab DFlash2 draft, which sits closer to the paper's regime than our 32k-vocab DSpark. We would run that only as a check, not as a planned stage, and only if the control arm is run alongside it.

6. What we keep

  • The infrastructure: data-parallel spec-decode rollouts via vLLM with per-slot accept logs, rollouts_to_dataset.py, the OPD loss with OOV masking, SFT-replay blocks, paired pool-acceptance measurement with a stop-on-drop guard, and multi-arm evals. Reusable for any on-policy data generation.
  • A cheap anneal. One round of ordinary SFT on 16k on-policy rollouts recovers a third of the gap between an early high-LR checkpoint and the end of its run (ep8: 5.47 → 5.67 on AIME). Useful if a GLM run has to be cut short; a proper LR anneal gives the same for free.
  • The control discipline. Pool acceptance on training prompts overstates gains; every on-policy change needs an ordinary-loss arm on the same rollouts and a benchmark eval at the served k.
